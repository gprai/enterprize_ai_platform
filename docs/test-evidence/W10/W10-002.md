## Business and architecture outcome

W10-002 adds an optional provider-neutral reranking stage after authorized
retrieval. The selected vector backend still produces complete
provider-neutral chunks. Every returned candidate is reauthorized before any
text enters the reranker. The bounded reranker may reorder only that exact set;
its response must contain every candidate identity exactly once, after which
the selected results are reauthorized again. It cannot introduce, restore, or
substitute a denied result.

The plain-language analogy is a second librarian who may reorder only books
the first librarian has already authorized. The second librarian receives a
small, fixed cart, works within a short deadline, and may not add a book. If
that librarian is unavailable, overloaded, late, or returns the wrong labels,
the original authorized order is used. The added delay must be justified by
measured relevance improvement and explicit cost evidence before adoption.

```text
authorized query and backend candidates
  -> complete returned-result authorization and identity validation
  -> bounded provider-neutral rerank request
  -> approved local deterministic reranker, when explicitly enabled
  -> exact candidate-set and identity validation
  -> authorization-preserving reordered top-k
  -> quality, latency, saturation, cost, and backend-difference evidence
  -> ADOPTED / NOT_ADOPTED / INDETERMINATE decision
```

Reranking is default-off in both `.env.example` and the Kubernetes ConfigMap.
AWS composition constructs the approved local reranker only when the typed
`ENTERPRISE_AI_RAG_RERANKER_ENABLED` setting is true. No unmanaged provider,
network lookup, model download, credential construction, or silent provider
fallback is present.

## Exact bounds, concurrency, failure, and recovery

The immutable W10-002 policy sets:

- at most 20 candidates and 20 returned results;
- at most 256 query tokens, 384 tokens per candidate, and 8,192 total tokens;
- one 250 ms reranker deadline;
- at most four concurrent reranker calls, zero queue capacity, and zero
  reranker retries;
- recall@5 of at least 0.85 and no regression from baseline;
- no precision regression, at least 0.05 MRR gain, and citation precision 1.0;
- zero authorization violations;
- at most 100 ms incremental p95 and 750 ms total retrieval p99;
- zero paid provider calls and zero measured incremental paid cost for the
  approved local identity.

The W10-001 hybrid backend contract remains two total attempts per channel —
the initial call plus one retry — and at most four backend search calls. W10-002
does not add a backend retry. It makes at most one local reranker call after
backend retrieval.

A fifth concurrent reranking request does not wait in a queue and makes no
extra reranker call; it returns the exact authorized baseline order. Timeout,
unavailability, saturation, or a malformed/unknown response also degrades to
that baseline. Cancellation propagates while releasing capacity. Input-limit
violations fail before reranker execution. Unauthorized or duplicate backend
results fail closed rather than degrading to broader access.

Qdrant and the in-memory backend remain vector-only, while OpenSearch can
produce hybrid candidates under W10-001. Reranking operates on the common
authorized result contract and does not pretend those candidate-generation
algorithms are semantically identical. These backend differences remain
explicit in comparison evidence.

## Security, privacy, reliability, and cost

- Authorization precedes reranking and is repeated after reordering. The
  reranker cannot add an identity because a response must be an exact
  permutation of the authorized candidate set.
- Reranker error classes expose stable non-sensitive codes and messages. Query
  text, candidate text, tenant identity, ACL metadata, credentials, and raw
  provider exceptions are not placed in errors or adoption evidence.
- Candidate, token, concurrency, queue, timeout, and retry limits bound memory,
  CPU, latency, and denial-of-service exposure.
- The approved implementation is deterministic repository source with no
  network or model-download requirement. No managed reranker or uncontrolled
  provider fallback exists.
- The comparison schema requires explicit known backend differences and keeps
  incomplete or synthetic evidence from becoming an adoption decision.
- Local workstation CPU, memory, disk, and owner time were used but not
  monetized. No AWS, hosted reranker, paid model, registry, deployment, or
  billing operation ran, so production cost remains unproved.

## Decision, alternatives, and adoption status

**Implementation decision:** provide a default-off, deterministic local
reranker behind a strict provider-neutral boundary. Preserve the complete
authorized backend candidate set, use bounded token overlap for the reference
ordering, fail closed on backend authorization/identity defects, and safely
degrade to the authorized baseline for operational reranker failures.

**Adoption decision:** **INDETERMINATE**. Automated tests prove the decision
function and its thresholds, including a representative-shaped fixture that
would return `ADOPTED`. That fixture is not owner-approved production evidence
and does not measure a real corpus, live backend, production traffic, or
monetized operating cost. The configuration therefore remains default-off and
no ADR records adoption.

Rejected alternatives are direct provider SDK coupling; a managed or downloaded
model without an approved identity; unbounded candidates/tokens/concurrency;
reranking before authorization; accepting partial or substituted response IDs;
retrying through a different provider; restoring denied results; masking
Qdrant/OpenSearch differences; or treating synthetic metrics as business
adoption evidence.

No new ADR is written in Column M. ADR-002, ADR-003, and ADR-006 already own the
provider, vector-store, and authorization boundaries. A task-specific adoption
ADR is deferred until representative owner-approved measurements yield an
actual `ADOPTED` or `NOT_ADOPTED` decision that changes architecture policy.
